# Fantasy Football Matrix™ Trademark & Brand Protection Plan

**Effective Date:** September 7, 2026

This document is an internal brand-protection plan for Fantasy Football Matrix™ and related Cactus🌵Byte Studios™ branding. It is not a trademark registration and does not itself create federal registration rights.

## 1. Core Marks to Protect
Primary marks:
- **Fantasy Football Matrix™** — product name.
- **Cactus🌵Byte Studios™** — studio/developer brand.

Potential secondary marks should be evaluated before public adoption, including distinctive names for proprietary features such as Season Intelligence, Roster Doctor, Waiver Assassin, Trade Hunter, Opponent Exploiter, Weekly Attack Plan, and Matrix scoring features.

## 2. Ownership Position
Brett Ross should maintain clear records showing authorship, first use, source-code ownership, branding files, domain/control records, release history, and commercial use of the Fantasy Football Matrix™ brand and original software.

Third-party names and marks—such as NFL, NFL club names/logos, player publicity rights, Sleeper, Yahoo, ESPN, Fleaflicker, and other fantasy-platform brands—must not be presented as owned by Fantasy Football Matrix™.

## 3. Use of ™ and ®
Use **™** with Fantasy Football Matrix™ and other unregistered marks where appropriate to signal a trademark claim.

Do **not** use **®** unless and until the specific mark is federally registered with the United States Patent and Trademark Office for the relevant goods/services.

## 4. Clearance Before Filing
Before investing heavily in branding or filing an application, conduct a trademark clearance search covering:
- USPTO federal registrations and pending applications;
- state business/trademark databases where appropriate;
- major app stores, domains, social platforms, fantasy-football products, and software marketplaces;
- confusingly similar names in related software, analytics, sports, and fantasy-gaming categories.

A qualified trademark attorney can provide a professional clearance opinion.

## 5. Federal Registration Strategy
If commercial traction justifies filing, evaluate USPTO registration for the word mark **Fantasy Football Matrix** and, separately if valuable, a distinctive logo/design mark.

Potential identification of goods/services should be drafted carefully around downloadable/software-as-a-service fantasy-football analytics, roster management, recommendation, and decision-support software. Final filing classes and descriptions should be reviewed by trademark counsel.

## 6. Brand Usage Rules
Maintain consistent spelling, capitalization, logo proportions, and attribution. Avoid genericizing the mark. Examples:
- Preferred: “Use Fantasy Football Matrix™ to analyze your roster.”
- Avoid: using “fantasy football matrix” generically to describe all fantasy analyzers.

Brand assets should retain original source files and dated versions.

## 7. Third-Party Trademark Hygiene
The product must clearly state that it is independent and not endorsed by or affiliated with the NFL, NFL clubs, Sleeper, or other fantasy platforms unless a written relationship exists.

Third-party logos should not be used in a manner that implies sponsorship, official status, or ownership. Nominative references to compatibility or data sources should be factual and limited to what is reasonably necessary.

## 8. Proprietary Feature Names
Before elevating feature names into standalone brands, check whether they are distinctive and clear of conflicts. Descriptive phrases may receive weaker trademark protection than distinctive marks. If a feature becomes commercially important, consider a separate clearance/filing decision.

## 9. Enforcement Records
Maintain evidence of:
- first public/commercial use dates;
- screenshots and release notes;
- source-control history;
- app-store/web listings;
- marketing materials;
- invoices/subscription records if applicable;
- reports of confusing copycats or impersonation.

Potential infringement should be documented before sending takedown demands or cease-and-desist communications. Escalation should be proportionate and reviewed by counsel where material.

## 10. Domain and Account Protection
Where practical, secure relevant domain names and official social handles, use strong authentication on registrar/source-control/hosting accounts, and retain recovery information. Avoid creating unnecessary domains or accounts solely for defensive purposes when they provide little value.

## 11. Copyright and Trade-Secret Layer
Trademark protection should work alongside:
- copyright protection for original code, text, graphics, documentation, and other creative works;
- proprietary licensing restrictions;
- controlled access to non-public source code and sensitive internal methods;
- appropriate confidentiality provisions for contractors or collaborators.

Ideas, facts, player statistics, third-party data, and generic fantasy-football concepts are not made proprietary merely by inclusion in the App.

## 12. Review Trigger
Revisit this plan before:
- paid public launch at meaningful scale;
- filing an app-store listing under a new mark;
- licensing the product to another business;
- adding major co-branding or third-party partnerships;
- filing a USPTO application;
- sending an infringement demand.
